# Ablegen & Aufstoppen am Brückenpfeiler

Study notes for two practical maneuvers from the German boat license exam (Sportbootführerschein). They cover **casting off from a pier** and **stopping and holding the boat in front of a bridge pillar**, with top-down diagrams and the German your instructor will actually shout.

The notes assume the usual training boat: one outboard or sterndrive engine and a steering wheel.

## Das Boot an der Pier

Every command refers to these parts. The nav lights help you remember the sides: **Backbord = links = rot**, **Steuerbord = rechts = grün**, always seen looking toward the bow (*Bug*).

![Boat moored starboard-side to a pier with bow line, stern line, bow spring and stern spring](diagrams/00-boot-an-der-pier.svg)

The **Vorspring** (bow spring) runs from the bow *backward* to the pier. The **Achterspring** runs from the stern *forward*. Springs stop the boat from sliding along the pier, and the Vorspring is what you use to cast off.

| Grundregel | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Kein Gas, keine Lenkung** | An outboard steers by pointing its thrust. In neutral (*Leerlauf*) turning the wheel does almost nothing. |
| **Kurze Gasstöße** | Short, gentle bursts of throttle, then back to neutral. Never long, strong throttle near a pier or pillar. |
| **Gegen Strom oder Wind** | Always make the final approach against the current (or the wind if there's no current). The water then slows you down and you keep control. |
| **Rückwärts: das Heck führt** | In reverse the stern goes where the wheel points and the bow swings the other way. Inboards also kick the stern sideways (*Radeffekt*). |

## Manöver 1 · Ablegen: Eindampfen in die Vorspring

This is the standard exam method. It also works when wind or current press you against the pier (*auflandiger Wind*). You motor forward gently against the bow spring. The bow stays on a fender and the stern swings out into open water. Then you back out.

In the drawings the pier is on **Steuerbord**. If it's on **Backbord**, mirror every wheel command.

### Wenn der Wind vom Steg weg weht

If the wind blows off the pier (*ablandiger Wind*), it's easy. Release all the lines and let the boat drift a metre or two. Then go slow ahead, parallel to the pier. Instructors sometimes ask for this short version: **„Leinen los, abtreiben lassen.“**

### Typische Fehler

- Too much throttle against the spring, so the bow crushes the fender. Use slow ahead only.
- Turning the wheel the wrong way in step 2. It goes **toward** the pier.
- Reversing before the spring is off, or while the boat is still in gear. Always shift to neutral first.
- Turning away too early in step 4, so the stern sweeps into the pier.
- Forgetting the all-round look (*Rundumblick*) before every movement. Examiners notice this.

## Manöver 2 · Aufstoppen vor dem Brückenpfeiler

The task: approach a bridge pillar, stop with the bow about one metre in front of it, and hold the boat still (*auf der Stelle halten*) until the instructor says **„Gut, steht.“** The key is to approach **against the current**. The current then acts as your brake, and small bursts of throttle hold you in place.

![Approach to a bridge pillar against the current, with the neutral point, stopping point and a mark on the bank used to hold position](diagrams/05-brueckenpfeiler.svg)

Current flows from the bridge toward you, so you approach upstream (*stromauf*, on rivers also *zu Berg*). The faint boats show where the bow is when you shift to neutral and when you check your speed.

### Gashebel-Ablauf (throttle sequence)

| 🟢 Langsam voraus | ⚪ Leerlauf | 🔴 Kurz zurück | ⚪ Leerlauf | 🟢 Kurz voraus ↔ Leerlauf |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Approach in line with the pillar | About 3 boat lengths before the pillar, let it glide | Only if still moving: 1–2 s | Boat stops about 1 m from the pillar | Pulses to stay put against the current |

### Schritt für Schritt

1. **Read the water.** Work out where the current comes from. Water piles up on the upstream side of the pillar and swirls on the downstream side. Also check the wind, other traffic and the bridge-passage signs.
   🗣 **„Wo kommt der Strom her?“**
2. **Line up downstream of the pillar**, bow pointing at it, a few boat lengths back. Take an all-round look.
   🗣 **„Rundumblick – gegen den Strom anfahren!“**
3. **Slow approach.** Go slow ahead or even just clutch in and out. Keep the bow exactly into the current, otherwise the current will turn the bow sideways (*der Bug fällt ab*).
   🗣 **„Langsame Fahrt voraus – Kurs halten.“**
4. **Shift to neutral early**, about 3 boat lengths out. The current slows you down quickly.
   🗣 **„Auskuppeln! Nimm Fahrt raus!“**
5. **Stop the boat.** If you're still moving toward the pillar about one boat length out, give a short burst of slow astern, then neutral. If you've stopped short and are drifting back, give a short burst forward.
   🗣 **„Kurz zurück – aufstoppen – Leerlauf!“**
6. **Hold position.** Pick a mark on the bank directly beside you (*querab*). If it moves forward, you're drifting back, so give a short burst ahead. If it moves back, you're creeping forward, so go to neutral. Make small wheel corrections only while in gear.
   🗣 **„Halt das Boot auf der Stelle.“ – „Gut, steht.“**
7. **Leave.** Look all around. Then back away slowly, or turn away with a little throttle and let the current help turn the bow.
   🗣 **„Rundumblick – langsam zurück – abdrehen.“**

> [!NOTE]
> **No current (lake, canal)?** Approach against the wind instead. Nothing slows you down for free, so you'll need the short reverse burst almost every time. On an inboard, reverse kicks the stern sideways (*Radeffekt*), so keep reverse short and correct afterward.

### Typische Fehler

- Approaching **with** the current. You can't stop and will hit the pillar. Never do this.
- Too fast, then a panicked burst of full reverse. Shift to neutral earlier instead.
- Staying in neutral too long while holding. The current turns the bow and you lose steering.
- Watching only the pillar. The distance to the pillar hardly changes at first, so use the mark beside you to judge whether you're moving.
- Big wheel movements. At this speed small corrections are enough.
